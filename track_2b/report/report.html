GemeindeSim: a bilingual civic what-if simulator on Apertus 1.5

Hack Apertus 2026 · Track 2B (own project) · Team hatif03 · 5–15 October 2026 · code (public): github.com/hatif03/gemeindesim, project root track_2b/ · judged endpoint: CSCS inference API · every number below is traceable to track_2b/research/ and docs/research/LAB-NOTEBOOK.md

1. Why this exists, and what we found

Problem. Swiss voters decide on written text, and the text can be wrong or misread. In 2019 the Federal Supreme Court annulled the 2016 Heiratsstrafe vote because the booklet said about 80,000 couples were affected when 454,000 were; a 2023 parliamentary audit found the booklet's four-eyes check is not always done by people with the right expertise; 43 % of 1,513 respondents could not say what the November 2018 vote was about (VOTO). In many of the 2,110 municipalities the assembly sees 4–8 % of eligible voters, so the written proposal is what most people read. We found no actor that pre-tests the wording of an official text with simulated readers before it is published (the closest: Singapore GovTech's Echo for government announcements, and UZH stance detection on booklets after publication). Evidence and sources: docs/PITCH-LANDSCAPE.md.

What we built. GemeindeSim reads a vote text in German and French, spawns a fictional town of up to 25 residents who talk, move, change mood and form a stance on a pixel-art map, and writes a closing report on who is affected and why – never how to vote. It is a what-if and pre-test tool, not a vote predictor. We also used the town as a test bench for Apertus 1.5 (8B and 70B, two endpoints): more than 20,000 logged calls and trials in 91 logs, 32 experiment scripts, 48 instrumented simulations.

What we found. Apertus is an excellent speaker (112/112 resident lines in the right language; it abstains when a premise is missing; no voting advice in 30 tests) and an unreliable decider: asked for a stance, all 15 residents said yes, even with no persona; on 54 real Swiss federal votes the simulated electorate was 34–37 points too favourable and the 8B followed an official recommendation 98 % of the time; both sizes defined Steuerfuss wrongly and failed a one-step percentage. The design follows: the model voices; the application owns the stance, the arithmetic and the sources. Effect (same seeds): valid citations 0/159 → 100 %, reports asserting an outcome 12/30 → 0/30, residents whose words match their stance ≈0.5 → ≈0.9, a town that starts with real disagreement. On a real 48-page Federal Council booklet the pipeline answered 12/14 questions correctly and abstained on every unanswerable one.

2. Architecture

Fig. 1. End to end. Blue: Apertus voices. Green: code owns. Red: code checks the model. Beige: user and screen.

Stack. Two Docker services: a FastAPI + LangGraph backend (src/backend: graph/builder.py standard and builder_swarm.py two-phase graphs; graph/corpus.py sentence chunking, BM25 with per-chunk language and the ballot question pinned, optional dense hybrid; graph/llm.py async client, adaptive limiter and streaming; graph/chat.py grounded 1:1 chat; graph/metrics.py token accounting; services/economic_report.py; routers/simulate.py Socket.IO stream, routers/ensemble.py multi-seed spread) and a Next.js + Phaser frontend (map, event feed with citation chips, stance poll, influence graph, report, run-metrics panel, replay recorder/loader). The corpus is a read-only volume; the only outbound call is the OpenAI-compatible LLM_BASE_URL. No personal data is used: residents are generated at run time (code draws name, role, income band, leaning, language; the model writes the persona text).

Honest status: all behaviours were measured on two hosted endpoints; no GPU was available, so no local run was measured (research/probe_endpoint.py runs the same capability table against any endpoint).

Fig. 2. Stance is computed in code (weights are assumptions, not calibrated).
Code-owned controls (each added after a measured failure, section 4):
  • Stance: ideology × leaning + 0.5 × judged impact − 0.6 × computed household cost + noise (sd 0.15); thresholds ±0.15.
  • Opinion dynamics: bounded confidence (Deffuant): keep / compromise / adopt by influence; only residents who conversed move.
  • Sources: passages carry labels [P#]; a citation that does not exist is dropped; the ballot question is always in the prompt.
  • Numbers: numeral gate strips figures not in the pack or the calculator; household CHF comes from a calculator on the booklet's worked example.
  • Speech binding: last paragraph of the prompt states the code stance (“your position: for/against”).
  • Report guardrails: no asserted outcome, no vote advice, Swiss spelling (no ß), local disclaimer.

Real booklet and spread across seeds. On the 48-page Federal Council booklet of 28 Sept 2025 (83 k characters, 209 chunks, two measures) the retriever puts the gold evidence into four passages for 11/14 questions (old retriever 10/14); the 70B answered 12/14 correctly (hand-graded), abstained on 3/3 unanswerable questions and abstained wrongly only where the evidence was not retrieved: retrieval, not the model, is the bottleneck. Personas and the stance prior are drawn per seed and T=0 is not reproducible, so one run is not a result: across five 70B seeds the final share for ranges 0.00–0.80 (8B 0.00–0.40), and the poll did not change in 4 of 5 runs within three rounds. Compare-conditions (E19, 8B, three paired seeds): adding the referendum committee's counter-arguments lowers the mean stance in all three seeds (−0.28); a yes/no council recommendation does not move it consistently, because the stance never reads it.

5. Second endpoint, engineering checks and the demo run

Fig. 7. Same code, same seeds. The hackathon gateway answers 429 above 4–5 requests in flight; CSCS answered 2,496 requests up to 96 in flight without one.

CSCS (E20–E28). Capability matrix, throughput, long context, determinism, structured output, stance on four models, retrieve-first vs whole-booklet, the final code on 5 seeds × 2 sizes, and the grounded chat live. All v2 fixes replicate (valid citations 1.00, no self-introductions, no asserted outcome, speech matches the code stance 0.85–0.88); so do the Swiss-knowledge and real-vote results. What does not change: the yes-bias (E24), one tool call, non-reproducible T=0. One build, both endpoints (E31): ids are mapped, and the limiter learned from three iterations (additive growth: 656 s on the busy gateway; slow start; a latency rule): 25 residents × 2 rounds took 89–97 s on CSCS, 654 s on the gateway that afternoon (more requests in flight added latency, not throughput there).

Mentor questions, answered with measurements (E29–E32)

Fig. 8. Python is 2.2–5.0 % of the wall time; output tokens decide a call's latency (regression on 464 logged 70B calls: 0.6 s + 0.0172 s per output token + 0.85 s per 1 k uncached prompt tokens, R² 0.76).
  • Python / async / context. Wall time is the longest chain of dependent calls (persona → impact → reflection → turn → translation) at ≈60 tokens/s; every call is async; resident prompts use at most 1.1 % of the 262 k window, so no compaction is needed. 17–25 % of prompt tokens were cached on CSCS; moving the instruction block to the front would save ≈0.3 s of a 5.5 s call but moves the speech-binding paragraph (E15): not done.
  • Streaming. The chat streams sentence by sentence, each sentence passing the number gate, bracket removal and Swiss spelling before it is sent: first text 1.5 s, whole answer 2.4 s (median, 25 answers), 0 invented figures. JSON turns and the report are not streamed (they must be complete to be validated).
  • Tokens and metrics. Counted from the endpoint's usage per run; a “Run metrics” panel and GET /metrics show tokens in/out/cached, latency p50/p95, context use, retries, endpoint, in-flight limit. Subagents / pagination: each resident is an isolated context run in parallel; no tool loop; every result set is a fixed small page.

Limitations (what we do not claim)

  • Not a vote predictor (54 real votes; weak signal after bias correction). Stance weights are modelling assumptions, not calibrated; the stance prior produces disagreement and is not evidence that real residents would split that way. Evaluation uses one synthetic town text and one real booklet.
  • Small n: 3–5 simulations per group, 15 residents in population tests, 14 retrieval questions, 15 held-out votes; ranges, no significance tests; LLM classifiers (79–91 % mutual agreement) score the speech tests.
  • Two hosted endpoints, a few days; latency drifts several-fold. Local and air-gapped deployment were not measured (no GPU). German/French register: a native-speaker review pack exists (docs/review/); we do not claim native review until corrections are applied.
  • Open: the price_pressure indicator is always 0; the 8B never emits move; the backend caps residents at 25; the Linden sample is synthetic and short; real-booklet French edition untested.

Reproducibility (what a judge runs)

git clone https://github.com/hatif03/gemeindesim && cd gemeindesim && cp track_2b/.env.example track_2b/.env, put the key in LLM_API_KEY (defaults are the CSCS URL and swiss-ai/Apertus-v1.5-70B), then make run (without make: cd track_2b && docker compose up --build). UI: http://localhost:3000 (second port UI_ALT_PORT, default 8080; change it if taken). Click LOAD and pick track_2b/docs/replays/linden_25x5_cscs.replay.json to replay the recorded demo run without calling the model, or PLAY, paste the text from data/steuerfuss_linden_balanced_de.txt and _fr.txt, and run. Offline tests: cd track_2b/src/backend && uv run pytest --deselect tests/test_e2e.py (146 tests). Every experiment is a script in research/ with raw call logs in research/results/; docs/research/LAB-NOTEBOOK.md records every decision including failed attempts; docs/ENDPOINTS.md and docs/ENGINEERING-QA.md hold the endpoint and engineering details.
