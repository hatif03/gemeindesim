Town of Riverside: proposal for a one-year bike lane trial on Station Road

The Town Council proposes a one-year trial of protected bike lanes on Station Road between the train station and the school campus, starting in April 2027.

What would change: On the 0.75 mile stretch, one car lane per direction is replaced by a protected bike lane. The 45 on-street parking spaces on the north side are removed; 20 replacement spaces are built behind the post office. The speed limit stays at 30 mph.

Costs: The trial costs $350,000 for paint, bollards and signs. If the town makes the lanes permanent, the rebuild would cost an estimated $2.3 million, of which the state would pay up to 40 percent.

Reasons given by the council: 23 children cycle to the school campus every day; two accidents involving cyclists were reported in 2024. Traffic counts show 7,400 vehicles per day, of which about a third are through traffic.

Concerns raised by the local business association and some residents: Delivery vans would block the single car lane. Shops on Station Road fear losing customers who arrive by car. Bus line 4 would slow down in the evening rush hour; the transit agency expects delays of up to two minutes.

Supporters point to the school route and the town's climate goals. Opponents ask for a trial on a shorter section first and for the replacement parking to be built before the lanes open.

This is a synthetic scenario (an English-language local proposal), not a real proposal. No voting recommendation is given.
